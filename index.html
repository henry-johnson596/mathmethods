Study Skills
⏱️
Review within 24 hours
If you don't revisit what was taught within 24 hours, you'll forget roughly half of it. Even 10 minutes of review the same evening makes a big difference.
✍️
Studying vs. Revising
Studying is active first-time learning. Revising is placing already-learnt knowledge into a test context. Don't confuse reading notes with actually practising problems.
🔕
Remove distractions
Unwanted noise causes your mind to wander — 78% of students experience this. Find a quiet space when working through maths problems.
📝
Do the problems
Maths is not a spectator sport. Close the book and reproduce the solution yourself. If you can't, you haven't learned it yet.

SAT 1 – Functions & Relations

Topic 1 · 60 minutes · 38 marks · Hard copy · Blue or black pen
What's covered

Features of Polynomials
Topic 2
Investigation

AST 2 – Investigating the Features of Polynomials

Topic 2 · Electronic submission · Submit draft on Daymap first
Aim
Use graphing technology to investigate the relationship between the degree of a polynomial and the number of turning points and points of inflection it can have.
What you need to do
  • Part A: Investigate at least three degree-4 polynomials with four distinct real linear factors — record roots, turning points, inflection points, and a small graph in a table. Repeat for degree-5 polynomials with five distinct linear factors.
  • Conjecture: Form a conjecture about turning points and inflection points for a degree-n polynomial. Support it by investigating a polynomial of your own choice.
  • Part B: Investigate polynomials with a squared linear factor and two distinct linear factors. Explore further forms of your choice.
  • Write-up: Present findings clearly — sequential order matters as each question builds on the last. Use correct notation throughout.
Process
Read the full investigation → review the exemplar on Daymap → answer in order → write up → submit draft on Daymap → action Mr Johnson's feedback → submit final copy electronically.
Graded on
Concepts & Techniques (CT1–CT4) and Reasoning & Communication (RC1–RC5) — A to E performance standards.

Semester 1 Examination
Topics 1–3
Exam

Semester 1 Examination

120 minutes · Scientific calculator required · One A4 (double-sided) notes page permitted
Structure
  • Section 1 — Functions & Relations (27 marks): Vertical line test with explanation, domain and range, sign diagrams from graphs and algebraic functions (e.g. f(x) = (x+3)(x−1), g(x) = 5(x−2)²/(4−x)), composite functions f(x²) and f(g(x)), circle equations, and determining constants from given conditions
  • Section 2 — Quadratic & Other Polynomials (33 marks): Quadratic formula, factorising to solve (including rational equations like 3x − 10/x = 13), discriminant analysis, axis of symmetry, vertex and intercepts, sketching, determining quadratics from roots and a point, intersection of curves algebraically, polynomial behaviour at x-intercepts, and a gutter optimisation problem (maximising cross-sectional area from a 30 cm sheet)
  • Section 3 — Trigonometry (30 marks): Radian/degree conversions (10π/3 and −281.3°), arc length and sector area (angle 1.22 rad, arc 8.66 cm), exact values of sin θ and tan θ given cos θ = −4/5 with π < θ < 3π/2
Conditions
Answer all questions in the spaces provided. Additional working on the reverse of each page — label carefully. All working must be shown. Scientific calculator required.

Optimisation of a Supermarket Item
Topic 5
Investigation

Folio: Optimising a Supermarket Item

Stage 1 Math Methods · Topic 5: Applications of Differentiation · Max. 8 pages · Electronic submission
Overview
Investigate the optimal dimensions of a common supermarket container (e.g. a Vegemite jar, soft drink can, or milk carton) to minimise its surface area given a volume constraint. Apply differentiation and optimisation to solve a real-world problem and present your findings as a report.
What you need to do
  • Choose a container — select a real supermarket item, research or measure its dimensions, and note any constraints.
  • Build a mathematical model — write expressions for volume and surface area (e.g. cylinder: V = πr²h, A = 2πrh + 2πr²).
  • Optimise — differentiate your surface area expression, solve f'(x) = 0, and verify a minimum using a sign diagram.
  • Experiment — test at least three different configurations or constraints and compare your results.
  • Develop a conjecture — what ratio or relationship consistently minimises surface area?
  • Write a report — Introduction, Method, Calculations, Results, Conjecture, Conclusion. Correct notation throughout.
Tools allowed
Desmos, Excel, graphing calculators, or other technology. Show clearly how you used any tools.
Graded on
Concepts & Techniques (CT1–CT4) and Reasoning & Communication (RC1–RC5) — A to E performance standards.

How Safe is Chernobyl?
Topic 6
SAT

AT2 SAT – How Safe is Chernobyl?

Stage 1 Math Methods · Topic 6: Growth & Decay · Technology permitted
Context
The 1986 Chernobyl disaster released more radiation than both atomic bombs dropped on Japan combined. This task uses the real decay equation for Caesium-137 — the primary radioactive material released — to explore exponential decay, logarithms, and inverse functions in a real-world context.
What's covered
  • Using the decay equation A(t) = A₀e^(−0.023t) to find remaining Caesium-137 today (starting from 120 tonnes in 1986)
  • Map scale calculations — converting from a 1:20,000 cm scale map to real distances (storage facility and fire station from reactor)
  • Radiation dosage modelling using an inverse power function, finding Gy at given distances
  • Topics also draw on functions and graphs from Topic 1
Conditions
Supervised in class. Graphics calculator and laptop permitted. Show all steps clearly. Use correct notation and units. If your answer is wrong and you know it, explain why and what the answer should look like.
Graded on
Concepts & Techniques (CT1–CT4) and Reasoning & Communication (RC1–RC5) — A to E performance standards.

Scientific calculator required · 60 marks total
Structure
  • Section A — 26 marks · Growth & Decay: logarithm statements, solving exponential equations (including eˣ and base ⅓), single logarithm simplification, removing logs from equations

Conditions
Answer all questions in the spaces provided. Show all working for full marks. Additional working on the reverse of each page. Scientific calculator required.
